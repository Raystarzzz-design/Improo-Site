01 — Introduction

What are GLP-1 medications?

GLP-1 receptor agonists — brand names like Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound — mimic a natural gut hormone that slows stomach emptying and signals fullness to the brain. For many people, appetite drops dramatically, sometimes to a fraction of what it was before.

This guide isn't about the medication itself, dosing, or whether it's right for you — that's a conversation for you and your prescribing doctor. It's about a question that gets far less attention: how do you eat well when you're barely hungry?

Key insight

This guide covers nutrition strategy only — not medical or dosing advice. Always follow guidance from the doctor prescribing your medication.

02 — Why it matters

Why eating differently matters on GLP-1s

When appetite drops this much, meals feel "full" after only a few bites — which sounds like an advantage, but it creates two real risks. First, with so little room left, it becomes easy to under-eat protein and key micronutrients simply because there isn't enough appetite left to finish a normal meal. Second, when the body is in a steep, sustained calorie deficit, it pulls energy from whatever tissue is available — and without enough protein and resistance training, a meaningful share of that can be muscle, not just fat.

In practice, this means the old approach of "eat until full" stops working as a guide for getting enough nutrition — because fullness now arrives long before nutritional needs are met.

With so little appetite to work with, quality matters more than it ever did. Every bite has to work harder.

03 — Suitability

Who is this guide for?

This guide is written for people currently using a GLP-1 medication for weight management or blood sugar control, and who want practical nutrition strategy to go alongside it.

This guide is for you if
  • You're currently taking a GLP-1 medication under medical supervision
  • You've noticed it's genuinely hard to eat enough protein in a normal-sized meal
  • You want to protect muscle and strength while losing weight quickly
  • You're looking for practical, food-first strategies — not medication advice
Talk to a professional first
  • If you have a history of disordered eating — rapid appetite loss can be psychologically complex
  • If GI side effects (nausea, reflux) are limiting what you can keep down — speak to your prescriber
  • If you're pregnant or breastfeeding — GLP-1s are generally not used in pregnancy
  • Any questions about dosing, side effects, or whether the medication is right for you — that's between you and your doctor
04 — Honest assessment

Pros & cons of a protein-first approach

These are the trade-offs of deliberately prioritising protein and nutrient quality while on GLP-1 medication — not the medication itself.

↑ Potential benefits
  • Helps preserve muscle mass and strength during rapid weight loss
  • Reduces the risk of a "thinner but weaker" outcome
  • Protein-forward meals are often more tolerable than heavy, fatty ones
  • Smaller, frequent meals can help manage nausea while still hitting nutrition targets
↓ Real challenges
  • Reduced appetite makes hitting protein targets genuinely difficult, not just a discipline issue
  • Some common protein foods (large portions of meat) can worsen nausea for some people
  • Eating now requires intention — hunger no longer reliably tells you when or how much to eat
  • Risk of under-eating overall calories and micronutrients if meals aren't planned deliberately
Persistent nausea or GI symptoms? These are common with GLP-1 medications, especially early on. Smaller, more frequent, protein-forward meals are often better tolerated than large ones. If symptoms are severe or ongoing, contact the doctor who prescribed your medication.
05 — Research

What does the science say?

The clinical trials behind these medications show how significant the weight loss can be — and why nutrition strategy matters just as much as the medication itself.

[1]
Semaglutide: substantial, sustained weight loss

The pivotal STEP 1 trial found that adults taking once-weekly semaglutide lost an average of roughly 15% of body weight over 68 weeks, compared to about 2.4% with placebo — a scale of weight loss that makes muscle preservation a genuine priority, not an afterthought.

Wilding et al. (2021). New England Journal of Medicine, 384, 989–1002. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2032183
[2]
Tirzepatide: even greater average weight loss

The SURMOUNT-1 trial found that tirzepatide led to even greater average weight loss than semaglutide in earlier trials, at the highest studied dose — reinforcing how much lean tissue could be at stake without a deliberate protein and strength-training strategy.

Jastreboff et al. (2022). New England Journal of Medicine, 387, 205–216. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2206038

Clinical reviews on medically induced weight loss consistently flag that a meaningful portion of total weight lost — not just with GLP-1s, but with any large, fast weight loss — can be lean muscle mass rather than fat, particularly without resistance training and adequate protein intake. This is the core reason obesity-medicine specialists now recommend pairing GLP-1 therapy with a protein-focused diet and strength training.

What this means for you

The medication drives the weight loss — but what you eat determines what kind of weight you lose. Protein and resistance training are how you keep the loss mostly fat, not muscle.

06 — Summary

The short version

  • GLP-1 medications dramatically reduce appetite, which makes it genuinely harder to eat enough protein and nutrients.
  • Without enough protein and resistance training, a meaningful share of weight lost can be muscle, not fat.
  • Small, frequent, protein-forward meals are often easier to tolerate than large ones.
  • This guide covers nutrition strategy only — always follow your prescribing doctor's medical guidance.
  • Persistent nausea or GI symptoms should be discussed with your doctor, not pushed through.
